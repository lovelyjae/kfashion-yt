## 📋 복붙용 순수 나레이션 (TTS에 한 번에)
If you shop for clothes in Seoul and skip one simple step, you're literally leaving money on the table. By the end of this video, you'll know exactly how to get it back — and how much you'll actually save. Let's get into it.

Here's the deal. In Korea, almost everything you buy includes a ten percent VAT — a value-added tax. But if you're a foreign tourist, you're allowed to get most of that back. It's called the tax refund for tourists, and it works on fashion, shoes, bags, and skincare. To qualify, you just need to be a foreign visitor staying less than six months, and not working in Korea. That's it. Most travelers either don't know about it, or forget to use it. Don't be one of them.

There are two ways to get your refund, so let's start with the easiest one: instant refund. If a single receipt is under one million won — that's roughly seven hundred US dollars — the store can take the tax off right there at the counter. You pay the lower price immediately. No paperwork, no airport lines. How do you know if a store offers it? Look for a 'Tax Free' or 'Instant Tax Refund' sticker near the register — it's usually on the door or the counter. One thing you absolutely need: your physical passport. Not a photo on your phone — the actual passport. The cashier scans it, and the discount comes off on the spot. Quick tip: your purchase has to be at least fifteen thousand won to qualify. So one small ten-thousand-won tee won't count, but almost any real outfit will.

Now, what if your receipt is over one million won, or the store doesn't do instant refunds? Then you use the second method: airport refund. Here you pay the full price up front, and claim the tax back before you fly home. It's three simple steps. One — keep every tax-free receipt the store gives you. Two — at the airport, before you check in your bags, find the tax refund kiosk and scan your receipts and passport. For bigger items, customs may want to see the actual goods, so don't pack them deep in your suitcase. Three — collect your refund, either in cash or back onto your card. Give yourself an extra thirty minutes at the airport for this — it's easy, but you don't want to rush it.

Okay, here's the part most videos won't tell you. That sticker says ten percent — but you will not get a full ten percent back. The refund companies take a service fee, so what actually lands in your pocket is closer to four to seven percent. On a hundred-dollar jacket, that's about four to seven dollars back. Is it life-changing? No. But across a whole shopping trip, it easily adds up to the price of another outfit — for thirty seconds of effort at the counter. And one update for 2026: the tax refund for cosmetic procedures has ended, but regular skincare and fashion shopping still fully qualify.

So where should you actually shop to make the most of this? The big fashion districts — Seongsu, Hongdae, Myeongdong — are full of stores that do instant refunds, especially the bigger brand flagships and concept stores. Smaller boutiques and thrift shops often don't, so save those for cheaper finds where the tax barely matters.
