If you only read one chapter before your trip, make it this one. Used right, the tourist tax refund pays for an entire extra outfit over a shopping weekend — and it takes about thirty seconds at the counter.

Here's everything you need, with none of the fluff.

## The basics (read this first)

In Korea, most retail prices already include a **10% VAT** (value-added tax). As a foreign tourist, you can claim most of that tax back.

**You qualify if you:**
- are a foreign visitor staying **less than 6 months** in Korea, and
- are **not employed** in Korea.

It applies to **fashion, shoes, bags, accessories, and skincare** — basically everything in this guide.

**The one thing you must carry: your physical passport.** A photo on your phone is *not* accepted for tax-free purchases. Keep your passport on you whenever you plan to shop.

---

## Two ways to get your money back

### Method 1 — Instant refund (the easy way)

If a **single receipt is under ₩1,000,000** (about US$700), the store deducts the tax **right at the counter**. You simply pay the lower price. No forms, no airport queues.

**How to spot it:** look for a **"Tax Free"** or **"Instant Tax Refund"** sticker on the door or by the register. Most brand flagships and concept stores in Seongsu, Hongdae, and Myeongdong have it.

**At checkout:** hand over your purchase **and your passport**. The cashier scans it and the discount comes off on the spot.

> **Minimum spend:** ₩15,000 per purchase. A single cheap tee may not qualify, but almost any real outfit will.

### Method 2 — Airport refund (for big purchases)

Use this when a receipt is **over ₩1,000,000**, or when a store doesn't offer instant refund.

1. **Keep every tax-free receipt** the store gives you.
2. At the airport, **before checking your bags**, go to the **tax refund kiosk**, then scan your receipts and passport. For higher-value goods, customs may ask to see the actual items — so don't bury them deep in your checked luggage.
3. **Collect your refund** — in cash, or back onto your card.

> **Give yourself an extra 30 minutes** at the airport. The process is simple, but lines can form during peak hours.

---

## The honest part: how much you actually get

The sticker says 10% — but you will **not** receive a full 10%. Refund operators deduct a service fee, so the **real cash back is about 4–7%**.

| On a purchase of | You actually get back (≈4–7%) |
|---|---|
| US$50 | $2 – $3.50 |
| US$100 | $4 – $7 |
| US$300 | $12 – $21 |
| US$500 | $20 – $35 |

Not life-changing on a single item — but across a full trip, it easily adds up to **one more outfit, free.** Worth the thirty seconds.

## Quick comparison

| | Instant refund | Airport refund |
|---|---|---|
| When | Receipt **under ₩1,000,000** | Receipt **over ₩1,000,000**, or no instant option |
| Where | At the store counter | Airport kiosk before departure |
| Effort | Lowest (automatic) | A few minutes per visit |
| Need | Passport | Passport + saved receipts (+ goods on hand) |

---

## Pro tips

- **Split large buys across stores** when possible — keeping each receipt under ₩1,000,000 lets you use the easier instant refund.
- **Shop flagships for refunds, boutiques for bargains.** Small thrift and boutique shops often skip tax-free service; that's fine, because their prices are already low.
- **Carry your passport every shopping day** — you can't claim retroactively without it.
- **Skincare counts**, so bundle your beauty haul into the same trip.

---

## 2026 update

As of January 1, 2026, the tax refund for **cosmetic procedures** has ended. **Regular skincare and all fashion shopping still fully qualify** — nothing changes for the shopping in this guide.
